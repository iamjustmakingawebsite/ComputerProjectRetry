Timeline: From Abacus to Modern Computers

Computers changed from simple counting tools into powerful devices used for learning, communication, gaming, science, aviation, and much more.

1100 BCE or Earlier — Abacus

Important people: Ancient civilizations

The abacus used beads on rods to help people count, add, subtract, multiply, and divide. It was one of the earliest calculating tools.

1642 — Pascaline

Important person: Blaise Pascal

Blaise Pascal invented the Pascaline, a mechanical calculator that could perform addition and subtraction.

1822 — Difference Engine

Important person: Charles Babbage

Charles Babbage designed the Difference Engine to calculate and print accurate mathematical tables automatically.

1837 — Analytical Engine

Important people: Charles Babbage and Ada Lovelace

The Analytical Engine was designed as a programmable mechanical computer. Ada Lovelace(I read a book on her) wrote an algorithm for it and is known as the first computer programmer

1945–1946 — ENIAC

Important people: John Mauchly and J. Presper Eckert

ENIAC was a very large electronic general-purpose digital computer. It used thousands of vacuum tubes and performed calculations much faster than earlier machines.

1951 — UNIVAC I

Important people: J. Presper Eckert and John Mauchly

UNIVAC I was one of the first commercially produced computers. It was delivered to the United States Census Bureau.

1970s–1980s — Personal Computers

Important people: Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, Bill Gates, and IBM teams

Personal computers brought computing into homes, schools, and offices. Examples include the Apple II and the IBM PC.

2000s–Today — Modern Devices

Important people: Engineers and developers worldwide

Today, smartphones, tablets, laptops, cloud services, and AI-powered devices make computing portable, connected, and useful almost everywhere.
